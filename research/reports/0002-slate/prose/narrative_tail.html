What the checks caught

The most important findings of this slate are not arms. They are leaks and defects that would have mis-scored everything, and each was caught by a check rather than by someone reading the code.

Main had a look-ahead, found the first time the audit ran. The climatology benchmark counted each outcome h months after its forecast date, before the observation it rests on was published: 32 to 400 days early. The pre-registration had said the slate halts until main is clean. It halted, main was fixed (ADR 0009), and the harness measured the effect: one-year skill +0.2128 → +0.2154, paired interval [+0.0021, +0.0028]. The leak flattered the benchmark, not the model, and no verdict moved.

A second, smaller one, found by a reviewer where the audit cannot look (technical debt D14). The registry says a recession month is known 400 days after it. NBER announces turning points, not months: every peak since 1990 was announced within 400 days (128 to 366), and every trough after them (474 to 662, all eight dates checked at nber.org). So the months just after each trough reached the benchmark and the rates early, on 8 of the 391 scored dates, for the two recession indicators only. The audit trusts registered lags, so it could not see this. Main and every arm read the same registry, so it cannot favour an arm. Fixing it is deferred until this slate closes, so the reference stays put.

Defects caught before they could mis-score anything:

  • The runner only asked for the 90% interval. No arm could have reached confirmed, and each report would have recorded that as a result.
  • A reviewed fix that was never wired in. ADR 0007's likelihood allowance was tested but never called. It is now wired in, and full recomputation of 35 fits and 11,730 rows proved it byte-identical.
  • The run summary borrowed its fallback count from another command's file, which would have given every arm a spurious categorical change.
  • The audit's own gap. It perturbed by label, not by publication date, so the classic one-month look-ahead inside a publication lag would have passed. It was closed with a test that the old rule passes and the new one fails.
  • The scorer's review parser, three times. A substring search would have voided a clean arm whose reviewer wrote "no leak found". A regex emptied every section. And the first real review titled itself with the arm's name, which the parser took as the arm's section and read as unreadable. Each failed safe, and fixtures now pin all three.
  • A run whose code was being edited while it ran. One arm's exit_codes.json named a commit whose code never ran whole. The runner now refuses a dirty tree and records whether it stayed clean. The scorer refuses any arm whose worktree differs from its run's commit, and it applied that to earlier runs too.
  • The audit tested one arm with main's state count. Its from-scratch run chooses the number of regimes by calling the sweep directly. The backtest decides it one layer up, and that is where A2 fixes four states. So A2's audit fitted six states and never ran A2's seeding. A2's own author found it after the run, and it is verified in the code. The audit logs show that every other arm's audit fitted the arm's own count, so only A2 was affected (D15).
  • A leak by design, in the blend arm. Main's benchmark is clean as a benchmark, because no forecast reads it. A6's reviewer found that blending it into the forecast gives D14 and the revised-values approximation (D5) a way into forecasts. The leak is tiny: at most 0.0047 on one forecast, and 0.0002 on the one-year difference. The rule voids A6 anyway, as written, and the debt table's benchmark row now carries the warning.
  • An arm stopped, correctly, before writing code. A6's brief said to stop if the climatology was ever missing, and it was missing at today's date. The cause was index coverage, never a scored row, and it was resolved with the rule submit() already uses.

What the research says

Three briefings in research/briefings/ fed the slate's design. Each number below was derived or fetched, not quoted from memory.

  • The one-year calibration gate cannot be passed reliably at this sample size. A perfectly calibrated forecaster fails experiment 0001's ten-bin calibration-error gate (below 0.10) about 91% of the time at n = 31, the honest one-year sample. The threshold was not moved. That is your decision, below.
  • Fitted recalibration on about 31 outcomes makes forecasts worse out of sample. Platt, beta and isotonic maps all did, which is why the blend arm uses fixed weights.
  • The model is not a business-cycle model. Its second eigenvalue, about 0.98, matches the published volatility and level chains, not the growth chains (0.83 to 0.93). Output growth fitted alone runs about three times faster, with a half-life of 12 months against 25 to 43 for inflation and rates. That was the motivation for A4.
  • The information horizon is algebra, not bad luck. Hamilton (eq. 3–4) and Krolzig (eq. 21) show that regime forecasts converge to the unconditional mean.
  • All Weather's primary sources define the quadrants over surprises, not levels. The widely quoted 55/30/15 split appears in neither primary document. Hence two quadrant arms: A2 over levels and A3 over surprises.
  • External yardsticks. Professional forecasters' recession probabilities beat climatology one quarter ahead (+0.13) but not three to four quarters ahead. A real-time yield-curve model scores about +0.06, and is negative after 1990.

What this does not show

  • Every arm was designed by someone who had seen main's results. Pre-registration closes the tuning path but not the idea-selection path, so confirmed in sample is the ceiling here. Out-of-sample confirmation comes only from forecasts registered forward, the first resolving on 2027-07-01.
  • The look-ahead audit trusts the registry's publication lags (D14 is the proof that this matters). Revised values (D5) remain an accepted approximation. And it sees information on or after its cutoff, not between an earlier forecast and the cutoff.
  • Ten-year evidence is about 2.2 independent observations. No ten-year verdict here is informative, and the ten-year bootstrap rejects a large share of its resamples because rare outcomes sit in rarely drawn dates.
  • ADR 0007's other promise, that a fall in likelihood abandons one restart rather than the whole fit, is still not true in code.
  • The machine hibernated for 4 h 44 min mid-slate (16:00 to 20:44). Every run paused and resumed. No number depends on it, and it is recorded because it looked like a hang.
