## New evidence: the 2026-09-25 durability review ("Regimes Versus Baselines")

Deterministic computations (E2) from a review session. None has been adjudicated, and its scripts were
not committed. Key findings:

1. **The benchmark is weak by accident.**
   - The climatology is an expanding mean over every resolved outcome since the *source series* begins:
     USREC from 1854, INDPRO from 1919.
   - The model's rates are learned only from 1950-12.
   - Against a climatology restricted to outcomes from 1950-12 onward, one-year skill falls from +0.2673
     to +0.1786, five years from +0.1194 to +0.0690, and ten years from −0.1624 to −0.2109.
   - Both recession indicators turn negative at one year:
     - recession within the year: +0.199 → −0.283;
     - recession in the horizon month: +0.375 → −0.033, area under the curve 0.29.
2. **A regime-free forecaster wins.** A two-state Markov chain on each indicator's own monthly condition
   has no regimes and no model inputs, just two rates. It is estimated from published months since
   1950-12, starts from the last published condition, and steps across the publication gap.
   - Against the pipeline's climatology it scores +0.3254 / +0.1353 / +0.0815 at 1 / 5 / 10 years.
   - Paired against the two-chain model at one year: difference −0.0582 (the chain is better), 90%
     [−0.0957, −0.0263], 98.33% [−0.1115, −0.0104].
   - The pipeline's own one-regime composition is within +0.0288 of the two-chain model, 90% [−0.0095,
     +0.0656].
   - The main single-chain model was no better than one regime.
   - A direct logistic (inputs plus the indicator's own level, L2 = 1, never tuned) scores +0.3439 at one
     year, but +0.0345 at five years and −0.0735 at ten.
3. **Where regimes help and where they don't.** The regime model wins where its inputs are the question
   (inflation; the zero lower bound via the bill rate). It loses where it never observes the thing asked
   about (unemployment, NBER recession dating) and where the answer is mostly persistence.
   `compose_point_in_time` ignores the indicator's current state entirely.
4. **The adoption holds.** Two chains over one chain, +0.0590, survives seeds, bootstrap blocks from 12 to
   60 months, leave-one-era-out, and the corrected benchmark (+0.0650).
5. **Smaller record errors.**
   - D5 was closed on the wrong series: the output-growth condition differs from final data in 0.99% of
     condition-months.
   - The debt register says the benchmark reaches "back to 1919"; for recessions it is 1854.
   - ADR 0010's +0.0590 is a pre-D14 comparison; against the current baseline it reads +0.0519.
   - 4×4 sits at the top of its candidate range.
   - Mean-of-ratios versus pooled Brier skill is a forking path at long horizons (10y: −0.1624 against
     +0.2351).
6. **The unadjusted CPI (CPIAUCNS) appears never revised.**
   - Its 1994, 2005 and 2015 archival vintages match today's series back to 1913.
   - Output vintages are usable from 1970-12.
   - Using it could move the honest start from 1994-03 to about 1972: ~255 more forecast dates, ~52
     independent one-year observations instead of 31.5, plus the stagflation era.
   - Caveat: on the 1971 burn-in window, one regime wins the held-out likelihood, so 0001's regimes-exist
     gate would fail for main's single chain (ADR 0008). Untested for two chains.
7. **The review's recommendations, in its own order:**
   1. Don't ship the two-chain model as "the model" yet.
   2. Register forward now, monthly, with four forecasters side by side.
   3. Register a successor rule (0007) for future claims.
   4. Get back 1972–1994.
   5. Point-in-time questions through a regime × condition chain.
   6. Observe what is forecast: unemployment and the term spread.
   7. An equal-weight blend with the condition chain.
   8. Run 0006.
   9. A second audit cutoff at 2022-03-01, and make the layer check fail.
   10. Replicate on another economy.
