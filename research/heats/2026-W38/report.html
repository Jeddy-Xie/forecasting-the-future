Decisions requested

10001's calibration gate (ten-bin expected calibration error below 0.10) fails a perfectly calibrated forecaster about 91% of the time at the ~31 independent one-year observations available, so the one-year failure is not evidence of miscalibration.

Very likely (80–95%) that the gate is uninformative at this n.Confidence moderate, because briefing 01's simulation assumes noise scales with independent outcomes.Cost S.Two-way door.

Options: (a) keep the gate and its verdict, and report the one-year failure as uninformative beside the gate's null distribution; (b) register a new experiment with a calibration test that accounts for sample size, pre-registered before it runs; (c) leave it as it is. Changing the 0.10 threshold is not one of the options. Moving an acceptance threshold after seeing results is this project's single forbidden act, and all three options leave it exactly where it was committed.

Basis: seeded simulation (seed 20260915, 20,000 repetitions), with a median error of 0.153 for a calibrated forecaster at n = 31. The script is saved at research/briefings/derivations/sim_calibration.py. It was re-run for this report and gave P(error < 0.10) = 0.09 at n = 31, matching the briefing. It carries its own caveat: twelve overlapping monthly forecasts that share one outcome window behave closer to one observation than to twelve. So the true failure rate lies between the n = 31 figure and the lower rates the simulation gives at larger n.

P1 · arxiv-2609.14758 · undived corroborated · proof: unproven

Fabrication After Tool Failure: Tool-Augmented Agents Assert Values Their Tools Did Not Return

Claim. Every number an agent reports as a result should be extracted from the tool's output by code; without that, it is likely (55–80%) that at least one of the slate's seven arm reports carries a number its tool did not return. The confidence is low, for the reason given under decision 2.

Why now. Published 2026-09-13; surfaced in the 2026-09-15 cs.SE feed. This week's research slate runs seven agents that each report a primary number from a tool's output. This session has already produced one instance: a council lens reported a test count it had not observed, caught only because the chair re-ran the suite.

Evidence tier: corroborated. Two independent domains: the paper, which measures 14.10% of responses asserting a value the tool payload could not support under a deployment-style prompt, and an instance in this repository's own review. Title and abstract fetched from arxiv.org and matched on 2026-09-15; not read past the abstract.

Proof: unproven. This is not a measured quality claim. Nothing has been run.

What it costs. S. Each arm's primary endpoint is read from paired.json by code, not typed.

What it displaces. The typed number. The agent writes around the value instead of writing the value. The only new surface is one extraction step per arm.

Rollback. Remove the extraction step and reports return to transcription.

Strongest objection (written by the report author; no red team in phase 1). As practised, the rule will guard the primary endpoint in paired.json. But the instance that corroborates it was a test count inside a review verdict, not a primary endpoint. Unless "every number reported as a result" also covers review verdicts, the rule would not have caught the very case that motivates it.

P2 · arxiv-2609.13299 · undived anecdote · proof: unproven

From Experiments to Decisions: Reusing Evidence in Autonomous Coding Research

Claim. When the orchestrator consolidates the seven arm results into one report, each arm's evidence tier should be stated separately and the summary never allowed to outrank its inputs; without that, it is likely (55–80%) that at least one arm is summarised more strongly than its evidence supports. The confidence is low: this rests on one study, read at abstract level, of coding research rather than forecasting.

Why now. Published 2026-09-10; surfaced in the 2026-09-15 cs.SE feed. The consolidation it warns about is the slate's next step.

Evidence tier: anecdote. A single study. It documents agents carrying forward conclusions an experiment does not justify, including a coordinator that acknowledges a novelty-only rejection and then summarises it as measured closure. Title and abstract fetched from arxiv.org and matched on 2026-09-15.

Proof: unproven. This is not a measured quality claim.

What it costs. S. One field per arm in the slate report.

What it displaces. Nothing is deleted. The new surface is one field per arm, plus one constraint: the summary may not state a stronger tier than the arms beneath it. That is defended as the smallest change that makes a summary checkable against its inputs.

Rollback. Remove the field.

Strongest objection (written by the report author; no red team in phase 1). A tier is only as honest as whoever assigns it. If the orchestrator that is told not to inflate the summary also assigns the per-arm tiers, the inflation moves one step earlier instead of going away. This is advice, not a check, unless each tier is computed by code from the arm's own outputs.

Pulse

  • The Metaculus API now requires authentication. It returns 403 "only available to authenticated users", and its RSS and feed paths sit behind a Cloudflare challenge (source check, 2026-09-15).
  • The International Institute of Forecasters' site, forecasters.org, is behind a bot shield. Its site, blog, Foresight and wp-json paths all return 403 whatever the user agent. The journal's feed stands in for it.
  • The Survey of Professional Forecasters' recession probabilities beat climatology one quarter ahead (Brier skill +0.13, n = 230) but not three or four quarters ahead (−0.03 and −0.06) (briefing 01, W7).
  • In the M6 competition only 23% of teams (38 of 163) beat the equal-probability benchmark, and those that did improved by less than 2.5%. The average of the top teams beat the best single team (briefing 01, W1).
  • A perfectly calibrated forecaster fails a ten-bin calibration-error gate of 0.10 about 91% of the time at n = 31, with a median error of 0.153 (briefing 01, W5, seeded simulation).
  • At about 31 outcomes, Platt, beta and isotonic recalibration make out-of-sample Brier worse on average, even for a model that really is miscalibrated. Only a strong prior toward the identity map is safe, and it gains about 0.002 (briefing 01, W5).
  • Superforecasters' edge is mostly discrimination, not calibration: calibration error 0.01 against 0.04 for everyone else, resolution 0.40 against 0.32 (briefing 01, W3).
